Two Different Jobs, Both Worth Doing

Gutter cleaning is the functional half: removing the leaves, shingle grit and packed debris that stop water from reaching your downspouts. Clogged gutters overflow behind the fascia, rot soffits, soak foundations and — in winter — feed ice dams that push water up under your shingles.

Gutter brightening is the cosmetic half, and it's the one almost nobody offers. Those vertical black stripes on white gutters aren't dirt and they won't come off with a house wash. They're an oxidized bond of road film and pollutants that needs a dedicated detergent and hand agitation to release. We do both.

Every job finishes the same way: downspouts flushed from the top, flow confirmed at the outlet, and all debris bagged and hauled off your property. We don't leave piles on the lawn.

FAQ

Gutter Questions

How often do gutters need cleaning?

Twice a year is the reliable answer for most homes — late spring and again after the leaves drop. If you have pines or heavy tree cover directly overhead, you may need more; a home with no nearby trees can often go once a year.

Why won't the black stripes come off during a house wash?

House wash detergent is formulated to kill organic growth. Tiger striping is oxidized road film and pollutant residue bonded to the aluminum — a different problem chemically, needing a different product and hand agitation. That's why brightening is its own service.

Do you clean gutters with guards installed?

Usually yes, though it depends on the system. Some guards lift off easily and go back on; others are screwed down or built into the roofline and add labor. Let us know what you have and we'll factor it into the quote.

What if you find damage up there?

We'll show you photos and tell you plainly what we saw. We don't do gutter repair or replacement, so there's nothing in it for us to exaggerate — you'll get an honest report and can take it to whomever you like.
