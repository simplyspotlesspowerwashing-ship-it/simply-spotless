You Paid For Every Kilowatt

Solar panels are the one thing on your roof whose entire job depends on how clean it is. A film of dust, pollen, pollution and bird droppings blocks the light before it ever reaches the cell — and unlike a dirty roof, this shows up on your utility bill every month.

Rain does not solve it. Rain leaves mineral deposits as it dries, and it does nothing at all for the baked-on ring of grime that collects along the bottom edge of each panel where water sheets off. That lower band shades a full row of cells, and because panels wire their cells in series, shading a small area can drag down the output of the whole panel.

We clean with pure deionized water and soft brushes — no detergents, no abrasives, no harsh chemicals that could damage anti-reflective coatings or seals. Deionized water dries without leaving spots or mineral residue, so the glass stays optically clear instead of trading dirt for water stains.

FAQ

Solar Panel Questions

How much output do dirty panels actually cost me?

It varies with your climate, roof pitch and how long it's been. Losses are generally modest in rainy regions with steep panel angles, and considerably larger in dusty, dry or pollen-heavy areas with shallow-mounted arrays. The honest answer is that your own monitoring app will tell you more accurately than any general figure — check your production before and after.

How often should panels be cleaned?

Once a year suits most homes. Consider twice a year if you're near agriculture, a busy road, construction, or you have significant bird activity or heavy spring pollen.

Will cleaning void my panel warranty?

Cleaning done properly won't — but the method matters. Most manufacturers explicitly warn against high pressure and abrasive or chemical cleaners, which is exactly why we don't use them. If you have documentation with specific requirements, send it over and we'll follow it.

Do you walk on the panels?

Never. Panels aren't designed to be stood on and microfractures in the cells can result. We work from water-fed poles, ladders, or safe roof positions off the array.
